# Campaign Brief → Strategy Deck Handoff Guide

This guide explains how to use a completed campaign brief as the direct
foundation for a strategy deck. Every section of the brief maps to a specific
deck slide. No new research or strategic thinking is required — the deck
is a visual presentation of decisions already made in the brief.

---

## Principle

The campaign brief is the strategy document. The deck is the presentation
of that strategy. The deck should NOT introduce new strategic decisions —
it should communicate the decisions from the brief in a format suited to
the deck's audience (founder, client, leadership, all-hands).

If something is not in the brief, it should not appear in the deck without
first being added to the brief.

---

## Brief Section → Deck Slide Mapping

| Brief Section | Deck Slide(s) | Notes |
|--------------|---------------|-------|
| 1. Campaign Overview | Slide 1–2: Title + Campaign at a Glance | Use campaign name, objective, launch window, and campaign goal |
| 2. Audience Summary | Slide 3: Who We're Talking To | Visualize primary audience; include pain points and motivations |
| 3. Core Insight | Slide 4: The Insight | Single slide; state the insight prominently; explain why it matters |
| 4. Positioning Direction | Slide 5: Our Angle | The strategic bet; why this angle vs. alternatives |
| 5. Messaging Framework | Slide 6–7: What We're Saying | Core message on its own slide; supporting messages + proof points on next |
| 6. Tagline Options | Slide 8: Campaign Tagline | Show preferred tagline; optionally show 1–2 alternates for discussion |
| 7. Channel Plan | Slide 9–10: Where We'll Show Up | One slide per channel cluster (paid / organic / owned) or one overview slide |
| 8. Execution Notes | Slide 11: How We Execute | Creative directions, content types, sequencing |
| 9. Key Risks / Watchouts | Slide 12: Risks & Open Questions | Honest; include what needs to be resolved before launch |
| 10. Final Brief Summary | Slide 13: Summary / Next Steps | Use the 5–8 bullets directly; add clear next actions |

---

## Slide-by-Slide Build Notes

### Slide 1 — Title / Campaign Identity

**Source:** Brief Section 1 (Campaign Overview)

Content to include:
- Campaign name
- Product / offer
- Launch window

Keep it clean. This slide frames everything that follows.

---

### Slide 2 — Campaign at a Glance

**Source:** Brief Section 1 (Campaign Overview)

Content to include:
- Business goal
- Campaign goal (specific and measurable)
- Campaign duration
- Budget (if appropriate for this audience)

Format as a simple table or 4 labeled tiles. One glance should give the
reader the full strategic scope.

---

### Slide 3 — Who We're Talking To

**Source:** Brief Section 2 (Audience Summary)

Content to include:
- Primary audience (who they are, what they care about)
- Top 2–3 pain points
- Core motivation
- Key objection

Format: keep it scannable — bullet points or a simple persona card. Avoid
paragraph prose on deck slides.

---

### Slide 4 — The Insight

**Source:** Brief Section 3 (Core Insight)

Content to include:
- The insight itself (prominent, large text)
- 2–3 sentences on why it matters

This is often the most important slide in the deck. It should feel like a
strategic discovery, not a bullet point list. Use a single bold insight
statement and support it briefly below.

---

### Slide 5 — Our Angle

**Source:** Brief Section 4 (Positioning Direction)

Content to include:
- The positioning angle
- Why it fits the audience
- Competitive whitespace (if available)

Format: can be a simple statement + 2 supporting reasons. Optionally use
a contrast frame: "Others say X — we say Y."

---

### Slide 6 — What We're Saying (Core Message)

**Source:** Brief Section 5 (Messaging Framework)

Content to include:
- Core message (large, prominent)
- Emotional angle (1 sentence)
- CTA direction

This slide is for the single most important thing the campaign communicates.
Do not put the full messaging framework here — that is the next slide.

---

### Slide 7 — Messaging Framework

**Source:** Brief Section 5 (Messaging Framework)

Content to include:
- Supporting messages (3 bullets max)
- Proof points (2–3 specifics)
- CTA

Format: two columns (messages + proof points) or a simple list. Keep text
tight — one line per item where possible.

---

### Slide 8 — Tagline

**Source:** Brief Section 6 (Tagline Options)

Content to include:
- Preferred tagline (large, prominent)
- 1 sentence on why it was chosen
- Optionally: 1–2 alternatives if the audience needs to weigh in

If the audience is leadership or client, present the preferred tagline
first and frame alternatives as "options explored." Do not present all
5 as equal choices unless a decision is still needed.

---

### Slide 9 — Where We'll Show Up (Channel Overview)

**Source:** Brief Section 7 (Channel Plan)

Content to include:
- Channel names
- Role of each channel in the campaign (1 line each)
- Primary content format per channel

Format: a channel grid or simple table works well. One row per channel.
Keep role descriptions to 10 words or less per channel on this slide.

---

### Slide 10 — Channel Detail (optional)

**Source:** Brief Section 7 (Channel Plan)

If the audience needs more depth on specific channels (e.g., leadership
wants to understand the paid strategy), add one slide per priority channel:
- Role
- Content / messaging
- Why it fits
- Key metric

This slide is optional. Use only if the deck audience needs this level of
channel detail.

---

### Slide 11 — How We Execute

**Source:** Brief Section 8 (Campaign Execution Notes)

Content to include:
- Creative directions / themes (2–3 bullets)
- Content types
- Sequencing (high-level: phases or weeks)
- Any critical dependencies

This slide is for the execution team, not the approver. Keep it concise and
action-oriented. A simple timeline or phase diagram works better than prose.

---

### Slide 12 — Risks & Open Questions

**Source:** Brief Section 9 (Key Risks / Watchouts)

Content to include:
- Top 2–3 risks or weak assumptions
- What needs to be validated before launch
- Who owns each open item (if known)

Format: a simple 2-column table (Risk | Owner/Action) or bulleted list.
Do not hide this slide — transparent risk communication builds trust with
leadership and clients.

---

### Slide 13 — Summary & Next Steps

**Source:** Brief Section 10 (Final Brief Summary)

Content to include:
- The 5–8 summary bullets from the brief (edited for slide format)
- 3–5 specific next actions with owners and dates if possible

This slide closes the deck with decision clarity. The audience should leave
knowing exactly what the campaign direction is and what happens next.

---

## What to Change for Different Audiences

| Deck Audience | Adjustments |
|--------------|-------------|
| Internal marketing team | Include all 13 slides; keep execution detail; use internal language |
| Founder / CEO | Condense to 8–10 slides; lead with business goal and insight; trim execution detail |
| External client | Lead with their goals; soften internal jargon; frame risks as "questions to align on" |
| Cross-functional (sales, product, marketing) | Add context on how campaign affects their function; be explicit about dependencies |

---

## What This Guide Does Not Cover

- Visual design (layout, color, typography) — handled by design team
- Slide production software — this is tool-agnostic
- New strategic decisions — if a deck slide requires a decision not in the
  brief, return to the brief and add it there first

---

## Quick Reference: Brief → Deck in 10 Minutes

If you need to move fast from brief to deck structure:

1. Copy Section 1 → title slide + at-a-glance slide
2. Copy Section 2 → audience slide
3. Copy Section 3 → insight slide (most important — invest time here)
4. Copy Section 4 → positioning slide
5. Copy Section 5 core message → message slide
6. Copy preferred tagline from Section 6 → tagline slide
7. Copy channel table from Section 7 → channel overview slide
8. Copy creative directions from Section 8 → execution slide
9. Copy top 2–3 items from Section 9 → risks slide
10. Copy Section 10 bullets → summary + next steps slide

Result: a 10–13 slide strategy deck with no new strategic thinking required.
Every slide traces back to a decision already made and documented in the brief.
